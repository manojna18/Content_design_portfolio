Poppy & Ember

As someone who loves both the written word and art, Poppy & Ember was born from a desire to connect the two.

Most places to read poetry online are cluttered, clinical, or both. This project was designed to be the opposite: a minimal, immersive interface that slows you down. A hand-painted poppy garden at dusk. Glowing fireflies, each one hiding a poem. Tap one and read.

The experience is intentionally nostalgic.

Role

Product design & content curation

Tools

Claude Code

Timeline

6 hrs

Challenge

Mood and words, hand in hand.

Digital reading experiences prioritize efficiency and volume. Poppy & Ember was designed to invite curiosity, slow, deliberate interaction, and emotional connection. The poems were hand-picked to evoke nostalgia and longing. The visual design imitates walking into a garden at dusk. Mood and words go hand-in-hand to deliver a curated experience for users.

Results

From browsing to wandering.

Imitating an art installation, Poppy & Ember reimagines how users encounter poetry. It shifts the experience from browsing to wandering. It demonstrates how interaction, illustration, and restraint can work together to create a more immersive and contemplative digital space.

6 hrs Idea to product

Design choices

The artwork is the navigation.

The interface is built around a hand-painted illustration to evoke warmth and tactility. The contrast between the dark sky and luminous fireflies guides attention without traditional UI elements, allowing the artwork itself to function as navigation.

Conclusion

Restraint was the lesson.

Poppy & Ember required a different kind of design thinking. Without lists, menus, or familiar navigation structures, the interface had to guide users entirely through visual cues and interaction.

The most important lesson was restraint. Every added element risked breaking the stillness of the experience. Decisions had to be intentional, and minimal.

If I were to take this further, I would explore how to balance that element of discovery with subtle guidance, ensuring users don't feel lost while still preserving the sense of anticipation.
